Events & Sponsorship · 29 Sep 2026

At Dream Weekend, the sponsors built the party.

Jamaica's biggest summer festival shows where event sponsorship is heading: away from logos and towards spaces people choose to be in.

When Dream Weekend moved to Montego Bay this summer, its 17th staging and its first away from Negril in 16 years, the drinks brands did more than hang banners. Red Stripe, Guinness, Smirnoff and Casamigos built the spaces guests actually spent their time in: premium lounges, cocktail bars, sampling stations, branded social areas and interactive installations, each shaped around a particular party's crowd.

That is the shift in Jamaican event sponsorship. A logo on a stage backdrop buys visibility. A space guests choose to stand in, drink in and photograph themselves in buys association. Nadine Hylton, a brand manager at Red Stripe, told the Jamaica Observer that patrons no longer separate an event from the brands that make it happen.

Her other point matters just as much for anyone planning a sponsorship budget. A brand needs a clear reason to be at a particular event, and the audience, the music, the timing and the mood all have to fit. The same activation dropped into a day party and a late-night concert will not land the same way.

What this means for your next sponsorship

  • Buy a role, not a placement. Ask what guests will do in your space, not only where your logo will sit.
  • Match the space to the crowd. Plan each activation for one audience and one mood.
  • Make it worth photographing. A lounge, a backdrop or a photo booth that looks good on a phone carries the brand far beyond the venue.
  • Leave with proof. Count samples, photo sessions, sign-ups and posts, so next year's budget conversation starts with numbers.
The takeaway

Sponsorship value in Jamaica is moving from being seen to being experienced. Design the space and the moment first, then place the logo.
