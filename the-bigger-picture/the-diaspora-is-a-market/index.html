Diaspora · 29 Sep 2026

Jamaicans abroad are paying attention. Are Jamaican brands?

The diaspora sent home more than US$3 billion last year. Government and marketers alike say Jamaican companies are not selling to it hard enough.

US$3.28Bsent home by Jamaicans abroad, April 2025 to March 2026

Money is the clearest measure of how closely Jamaicans overseas stay tied to home. Bank of Jamaica figures show US$3.28 billion in net remittances in the year to March 2026, up 4.2%, equal to roughly 15% of the economy. In March alone, about 70% came from the United States, 11% from the United Kingdom and 8% from Canada.

That is an audience with income, loyalty and a daily appetite for Jamaican culture. Government is pointing companies towards it. In May, State Minister Delano Seiveright and JAMPRO president Shullette Cox urged Jamaican firms to pursue the UK market, citing the size of the Caribbean community there and its familiarity with Jamaican products. Grace Foods was the example held up: its UK business holds more than a third of Britain's Caribbean food market.

Getting onto shelves abroad is only half of it. Shannon Castonguay, the Jamaican-born founder of Toronto-based agency Adion Communications, told the Jamaica Observer in January that Caribbean products are gaining export traction but very few brands invest in sustained marketing overseas. The gap, she argued, gets filled by imitators trading on Caribbean imagery.

Reaching the diaspora from Kingston

  • Use creators who already reach them. Jamaican creators' audiences often stretch well beyond the island. On our own roster, between 35% and 42% of some creators' followers are outside Jamaica.
  • Plan around the moments that bring people home. Summer, Christmas and festival season are natural points to be in front of them.
  • Sound like home. Authenticity is the one thing imitators cannot copy.
The takeaway

The diaspora is already buying Jamaica. Treat it as a market with its own plan, budget and voice, not as a lucky spillover.
