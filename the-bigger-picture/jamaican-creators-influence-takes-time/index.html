Creator Economy · 29 Sep 2026

Jamaica's creators have a message for brands: influence takes longer than a weekend.

Local creators say one-off posts judged on next-day sales are wasting marketing money. The global numbers suggest brands elsewhere have already worked that out.

One sponsored post, and the soda is expected to sell out by Monday. That is the expectation lifestyle creator Rushane "RushCam" Campbell described running into with some Jamaican brands, speaking on a creator panel reported by the Jamaica Observer this year. It does not match how people actually decide what to buy.

The creators alongside him made the same case from different angles. Media personality Khadine "Miss Kitty" Wilkinson said a campaign's effect can show up months later. Singer-turned-creator Tami Chin Mitchell argued that Jamaican consumers need far more contact with a brand than the classic marketing "rule of seven" before they buy. Personal branding coach Naomi Garrick was the bluntest: one-off posts and two-week bursts waste money.

Campbell pointed to a Caribbean example that proves the point. A Crop Over festival partnership he worked on in Barbados ran from 2022 to 2024 and reached sell-out status in its third year, after the audience had seen it build season after season.

Bigger markets are already budgeting this way. EMARKETER forecasts US brands will spend US$21.1 billion on creator marketing in 2026, more than double the 2022 figure, with more of it going to smaller creators and to content reused beyond social feeds.

How to brief creators for results that show

  • Commit for a season, not a post. Three months or more lets the audience meet the brand repeatedly.
  • Agree the measures before the first post. Reach and engagement early on; clicks, codes or redemptions over time.
  • Brief for the creator's voice. Content that sounds like the creator outperforms content that sounds like an ad.
  • Secure the rights you need. If the content will run as a paid ad or on a screen, agree usage rights in the brief.
The takeaway

Buy creator partnerships the way you buy any media: with enough frequency to work, and with the measures agreed before the first post.
